For purposes of record we give hereunder a summary

in the death of Hassan bin Abdulla of Sitra.

From information given by Ridhy bin Abdulla and
Hamid bin Abdulla, brother and brother-in-law of the deceased,
it appears that all three men were proceeding inland from
Sitra early on Friday, 21st March 1947. At about 7.0 A.M.,
they entered Company property by going through the 4-strand
barbed wire fence enclosing the pitch-pond area. Hassan bin
Abdulla, deceased, apparently decided to take a further short-
cut by crossing the pitch-pond itself and had walked more than
half-way across when he went through the surface crust and
became trapped in the pitch. One of his compenions arrived
at the Lime Kiln at about 8.15 A.M., with news of the accident,
which was relayed through the Shift Foreman to the Assistant
Refinery Superintendent, Mr. H. Catlow. These men went straight
to the pitch-pond and immediately began attempts to extricate
Hassan bin Abdulla. Messrs. R.M. Brown, J.O. Fifer and R.A.
Andresen also went to the scene and remained there throughout.
The Inspector of State Police was informed and he came to
witness and assist the efforts to rescue Hassan bin Abdulla.
Every possible means of rescue was attempted but proved
unsuccessful. Hassan was immobilized in the pitch too far from
the nearest bank, approximately 800 feet, to permit the throwing
or shooting of a line to him. We stretched a greased rope the
entire width of the dyke of the pitch-pond, some 1700 feet, and
attempted to get it within his reach by moving up both sides
of the pitch-pond but the rope could not be hauled over the
surface of the pitch, and sank of its own weight into the pond.
A flat-bottomed float was about to be tried but the results of
the rope attempts showed that this would be quite impossible as
the pitch could not support it. About 1.0 P.M. three hundred
55 gallon drums and wooden planks arrived and an attempt to
build a pier out to Hassan was about to be made, when he sank
out of sight in the pitch. An R.A.F. plane flew over the pitch-
pond several times at approximately 2.0 P.M., but the occupants
confirmed that there was no signs of life.

Political Agency,
Kuwait,
6th February 1917.

My dear Sir Percy,

I enclose a memo of my interview with Mulla Salih whom I
sent for on receipt of news of the death of Shaikh Jabir.

There was only a very short period of uncertainty as to the
succession for Salim promptly took command on his own initiative
and ordered business to proceed as usual (i.e. the shops to re-
-main open) and fixed the funeral for the following day. He has
since conveyed me the fullest assurances as regards his future
policy. He volunteered that when notifying Shaikh Jabir's death
he will not address Bin Rashid and he will bring me any communi-
-cations he should receive from that quarter or any other party
with whom the British Government is not on friendly terms.

As far as it is safe to prophecy I feel that Salim being a
man of character and some constancy will be an improvement upon
Jabir. His austerity is not likely to tone down very greatly at
his time of life and we must bear in mind the limitations of a
straightlaced Mohammadon with Wahabi leanings in our dealings
with him. He says he wants my advice and I have begun by suggest
-ing that these young Sheikhs of the as Sabah family will be far
better employed in the desert looking after the tribes and main-
-taining order in the hinterland than in loafing about in town
with their bravos and pimps.

Jabir was too easy going and the country close to Kuwait was
gradually becoming unsafe. We want a bold policy. I venture to
suggest that two favours may be conceded at the beginning of his
regin firstly that Othman al Rashid be delivered to him for
punishment

The Hon'ble Lt-Colonel Sir P.Z.Cox, K.C.S.I., K.C.I.E.,
Political Resident in the Persian Gulf and
Chief Political Officer, Basrah.
